Biography

Weeratouch “Wave” Pongruengkiat

Artist Statement

I am an interdisciplinary artist based in Chiang Mai. I work with light, instruments and living systems to make physical processes perceptible that would otherwise stay hidden.

I trained as an engineer. My degree is in nanoengineering and my early research was in optics, including work published in 2017 on whispering-gallery-mode resonators for cancer detection. I build the instruments I work with, treating engineering as a method I inherit rather than a credential I wear.

My work began at the seam between art, science and technology.

The first sustained direction was biological. In Observing and Being Observed (2022) I built a microscope-and-projection installation from soil and living cultures, turning the act of looking at living matter back onto the viewer. In Living Vinyl (2023) petri-dish cultures became a record to be read as sound. I organised and led Intimacy with Nature, a bioart workshop at the Singapore Art Museum in 2023, and took part in the BioFeral BeachCamp at TTTlabs in Crete in 2024. I still run DIY-biology workshops where people who are not scientists meet living systems by hand.

Alongside this I work as a creative technologist on other artists' projects. I built the technology for How to Explain Sleep to Robot Cocks? with Henry Tan, shown in Hong Kong and at ZER01NE Day in Seoul in 2024, and for Khwan Dance, created by Padung Jumpan, presented at the National Asian Culture Center in Seoul in 2025 and on tour in Thailand.

In 2024 the work moved toward particle physics. Wish Upon the Passing Astroparticles, made for Awakening Chiang Mai, was an installation driven by a live cosmic-ray muon detector, built with the Thai-IceCube Collaboration at Chiang Mai University. It was the first time I worked inside a physics research group rather than beside one.

Quantum came after that, and has been the centre of the practice since. KHWANTUM (2026) is a lecture-performance made with Nitipat Pholchai and Wendi Wu, in which a Thai animist sense of beings meets the quantum field. This year I ran the ArtScience Entanglement Lab with Nitipat Pholchai, a workshop series asking how quantum happenings can be archived: four sessions with Media Arts students at the Syntopia Symposium, Chiang Mai University, and two public rounds at a science communication festival in Bangkok.

I am a core member of tomorrow.lab, a Chiang Mai art-science collective, and in 2026 organised the Arai-Eek Lab residency, a six-week programme linking the Hackteria and SGMK networks with local practitioners, funded by Pro Helvetia. I teach TouchDesigner at Sripatum University and have taught at NYU Abu Dhabi. What holds all of it together is an interest in things that are real but cannot be seen directly, and in the instruments people build to reach them.
